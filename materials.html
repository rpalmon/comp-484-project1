Types of Materials

Types of Materials:

  • Filament (FDM)
    1. PLA (Polylactic Acid)
    2. ABS (Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene)
    3. PETG (Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol)
    4. TPU (Thermoplastic Polyurethane)
  • Resin (SLA)
  • Powder (SLS)

Filaments

PLA (Polylactic Acid)

1 out of 5 Easy

Properties

  • Low printing temperature 180-220°C

Best Uses

  • Prototyping
  • Decorative items
  • Educational projects or anything really

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Easy to print, biodegradable, low warping, doesnt require an enclosure
  • Cons: Brittle, not heat resistant, not the best for functional parts

ABS (Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene)

4 out of 5 Hard

Properties

  • Higher printing temperature 220-250°C
  • Requires a heated bed and enclosure

Best Uses

  • Functional parts
  • Mechanical components
  • Outdoor applications

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Stronger and more durable than PLA, better heat resistance
  • Cons: More difficult to print, emits fumes, prone to warping

PETG (Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol)

3 out of 5 Medium

Properties

  • Printing temperature 220-250°C
  • Requires a heated bed but no enclosure needed
  • Combines the best of PLA and ABS

Best Uses

  • Functional parts that need durability
  • Mechanical parts and tools
  • Containers or anything that holds liquids
  • Parts that need chemical resistance

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Strong and durable, good layer adhesion, more flexible than PLA but not as much as TPU, food safe when done right
  • Cons: Can be stringy if settings are wrong, sticks to everything when hot, needs good cooling

TPU (Thermoplastic Polyurethane)

5 out of 5 Very Hard

Properties

  • Printing temperature 220-250°C
  • Requires a heated bed but no enclosure needed
  • Highly flexible and elastic material

Best Uses

  • Phone cases and protective covers
  • Shoe insoles or grips
  • Flexible hinges and moving parts
  • Anything that needs to bend or compress

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Super flexible, durable, shock absorbent, and really fun to print once you get it working
  • Cons: Very difficult to print, requires slow speeds, prone to stringing, not all printers can handle it

Other Materials

Resin (SLA)

4 out of 5 Hard

Properties

  • Uses liquid resin cured by UV light
  • Requires post-processing (washing and curing)
  • Produces extremely detailed and smooth prints
  • Resin can be messy and has strong fumes

Best Uses

  • Miniatures and figurines with fine details
  • Jewelry prototypes
  • Dental and medical models
  • Anything that needs really smooth surfaces

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Amazing detail and surface quality, great for small intricate parts, faster than FDM for small prints
  • Cons: Messy to work with, requires ventilation, post-processing is annoying, resin is more expensive than filament, smaller build volumes

Powder (SLS)

5 out of 5 Very Hard (Industrial)

Properties

  • Uses a laser to fuse powder particles together
  • Typically requires industrial equipment
  • Very expensive compared to FDM and SLA
  • Produces strong, functional parts

Best Uses

  • Industrial prototypes and end-use parts
  • Complex geometries that can't be done with other methods
  • Parts that need high strength and durability
  • Production runs of functional parts

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Very strong parts, no support structures needed, can print complex shapes that would be impossible with FDM
  • Cons: Extremely expensive, requires industrial machines, not practical for hobbyists, powder can be messy
