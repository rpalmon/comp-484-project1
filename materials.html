Filaments

PLA (Polylactic Acid)

1 out of 5 Easy

Properties

  • Low printing temperature 180-220°C

Best Uses

  • Prototyping
  • Decorative items
  • Educational projects or anything really

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Easy to print, biodegradable, low warping, doesnt require an enclosure
  • Cons: Brittle, not heat resistant, not the best for functional parts

ABS (Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene)

4 out of 5 Hard

Properties

  • Higher printing temperature 220-250°C
  • Requires a heated bed and enclosure

Best Uses

  • Functional parts
  • Mechanical components
  • Outdoor applications

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Stronger and more durable than PLA, better heat resistance
  • Cons: More difficult to print, emits fumes, prone to warping

PETG (Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol)

3 out of 5 Medium

Properties

  • Printing temperature 220-250°C
  • Requires a heated bed but no enclosure needed

Best Uses

  • Flexible parts
  • Prototypes requiring flexibility
  • Applications needing durability and flexibility

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Flexible, durable, and easy to print
  • Cons: Can be difficult to get right, may require post-processing

TPU (Thermoplastic Polyurethane)

5 out of 5 Very Hard

Properties

  • Printing temperature 220-250°C
  • Requires a heated bed but no enclosure needed

    • Best Uses

    • Flexible parts
    • Prototypes requiring flexibility
    • Applications needing durability and flexibility

    Pros and Cons

    • Pros: Highly flexible, durable, and suitable for complex geometries
    • Cons: Difficult to print, requires careful calibration, may be prone to stringing
